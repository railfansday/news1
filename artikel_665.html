Inside King Charles' Complicated Relationship With Ex-Prince Andrew

King Charles has signaled blood is only so thick when it comes to his brother Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and preserving the monarchy.

When Queen Elizabeth II died in 2022, King Charles III inherited the conundrum of what to do about his problematic younger brother, then known as Prince Andrew.

Not least out of a sense of necessity, Charles has since taken a series of steps to distance the royal now known as Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor from the monarchy, including stripping him of his title of prince a year ago.

And when his now 67-year-old sibling was arrested Feb. 19 on suspicion of misconduct while in public office, Charles said "the law must take its course."

Andrew was said to have been accused of sharing confidential information with Jeffrey Epstein—the disgraced American financier who died by apparent suicide in 2019 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges—while serving as a British trade envoy.

The law's course has since led to police admitting that the search warrants they obtained for Andrew's home were unlawful, a London judge said Oct. 8, but his relationship with his older brother has become no less complicated.

Andrew has denied all accusations of wrongdoing, but stepped away from royal duties in November 2019 after his disastrous appearance answering questions about his friendship with Epstein on BBC's Newsnight left viewers baffled as to why higher-ups at the Firm—including his mother the queen—sanctioned the sit-down in the first place.

Yet Andrew remained in the public eye, if no longer a senior working royal who carried out at least 400 engagements the year before he was forced into retirement.

When Charles first became king, however, he had other pressing matters to attend to, not least being the job of reassuring the United Kingdom that the monarchy would sail along just as smoothly with him at the helm as it did for most of his mother's 70-year reign.

And so Andrew kept attending high-profile events, most recently standing near Charles, Princess Anne, Prince William and Kate Middleton at the Sept. 16, 2025, funeral for the Duchess of Kent.

Meanwhile, William and Kate—who were said to be "deeply concerned by continuing revelations" in the Epstein files being released at the time by the U.S. Justice Department—signaled their support for the king in the wake of Andrew's arrest.

But considering the "disturbing narrative" around him, Andrew had already been "a liability for quite some time," royals correspondent Sharon Carpenter told E! News when he announced Oct. 17, 2025, that he would no longer use his Duke of York title or other remaining honors (though he was still known as Prince Andrew for two more weeks.

Andrew's most prominent title had been Duke of York, bestowed on him by his mother when he married Sarah Ferguson—now the former Duchess of York—in 1986.

"In discussion with The King, and my immediate and wider family, we have concluded the continued accusations about me distract from the work of His Majesty and the Royal Family," Andrew stated. "I have decided, as I always have, to put my duty to my family and country first."

The palace then announced Oct. 30 that Charles had "initiated a formal process to remove the Style, Titles and Honours of Prince Andrew," rendering him Andrew Mountbatten Windsor. (He remains eighth in line to the throne and daughters Princess Beatrice and Princess Eugenie's titles are not affected.)

Andrew also had to surrender his lease on Royal Lodge—the 30-room Great Windsor Park mansion where he'd been living effectively rent-free since paying for renovations in 2004—and move into a private home. Sarah, who'd been living with her ex-husband since 2008, was expected to figure out her own accommodations.

Andrew's new residence is at the king's Sandringham estate in Norfolk, and that's where Thames Valley police gathered the morning of Feb. 19 to take him into custody.

Police confirmed an arrest had been made, but noted in a statement they would not be naming the arrested man, "per national guidance," and advised "that this case is now active so care should be taken with any publication to avoid being in contempt of court."

Charles released a statement about two hours later saying he had "learned with the deepest concern" about his brother's arrest and the allegations against him.

"What now follows is the full, fair and proper process by which this issue is investigated in the appropriate manner and by the appropriate authorities," the king continued. "In this, as I have said before, they have our full and wholehearted support and co-operation. Let me state clearly: the law must take its course.”

"As this process continues,” Charles concluded. "it would not be right for me to comment further on this matter. Meanwhile, my family and I will continue in our duty and service to you all."

Despite the action the queen took toward the end of her life, including stripping the son rumored to be her favorite of his military titles, Andrew had a stalwart supporter in his mother. Though she signed off on his resignation, it was considered no accident that the pair were spotted riding together two days after he announced his step back from royal duties in 2019.

But a similar sign of solidarity from Charles was not to be expected.

"Andrew’s presence has been a thorn in the side of the royal family and a continued drag on the reputation of the institution," Carpenter said last October. And being relegated to going by Mountbatten-Windsor was a clear message from Charles that he "will not tolerate persistent scandals that put the future of the monarchy in jeopardy. His focus is on prioritizing the stability of the institution over family obligations or expectations."

And when it came to Andrew, the only remaining option was to get him literally out of the picture.

Incidentally, it wasn't until Andrew was born in 1960 that Mountbatten-Windsor—combining the queen and Prince Philip's family names—was established as a surname for their non-titled, direct male-line descendants.

And yet neither the late queen nor her husband could have foreseen that their third of four children would have to use that name 65 years later at the insistence of his older brother.

As Carpenter said, "The decision has come now because it had to."

Noting the "mounting pressure" Charles was under to distance himself from Andrew, she called the king's move the "nail in the coffin."

While Andrew's personal relationships with family members are "likely more strained than ever," Carpenter explained, "without a dramatic change in public sentiment, there can be no return to public duties. From this day forth, he is technically a private individual who happens to have royal lineage."
