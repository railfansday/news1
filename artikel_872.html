Capillary-selective vasodilation through myosin inhibition halts ischaemic cascades

Nature (2026) Cite this article

In acute myocardial infarction and acute kidney injury, capillary no-reflow denotes that capillary blood flow is still blocked despite successful arterial reperfusion. This is associated with substantially increased mortality and reinfarction risk; nevertheless, no targeted therapy exists so far1,2. Because capillary no-reflow is driven by smooth muscle myosin-mediated hypercontraction of capillary pericytes, we developed R(+)2501, a myosin-specific and capillary-selective vasodilator. In reperfused animal models of acute myocardial infarction and acute kidney injury, direct in vivo imaging showed that R(+)2501 rapidly restored capillary flow, an effect sustained even after rapid drug clearance. Ex vivo experiments confirmed reversal of pericyte constriction directly. Restoration of capillary blood flow, together with clearance of trapped neutrophils, linked resolution of capillary no-reflow to attenuation of an early driver of reperfusion injury. R(+)2501 enhanced capillary perfusion while preserving coronary autoregulation and systemic haemodynamics, and markedly improved survival during acute reperfusion. We further found that acute myocardial infarction induced renal capillary no-reflow, which was reversed by R(+)2501, suggesting that capillary no-reflow contributes to the pathophysiology of cardiorenal syndrome type I. These findings establish pericyte-targeted smooth muscle myosin inhibition as a therapeutic strategy to restore microvascular perfusion and halt the ischaemic cascade across organs, while defining a specific physiological role for capillaries and pericytes in circulatory regulation.

Representative time-lapse fluorescence video of a capillary segment ensheathed by a pericyte. Capillaries were labelled with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated WGA, and pericytes were visualized in transgenic NG2-DsRed BAC mice, in which they exhibit red fluorescence enabling unambiguous identification. Application of ET-1 elicits a rapid and marked pericyte contraction, accompanied by constriction of the capillary lumen. Subsequent addition of compound R(+)2501 induces an immediate relaxation of the pericyte, restoring capillary diameter and flow. These observations also support a direct, pericyte-specific action of R(+)2501.

Acute effect of R(+)2501 after 60 min of renal ischaemia. Within 10 s of intravenous injection, R(+)2501 dilates the peritubular renal capillaries and restores healthy perfusion to the pre-ischemic state (video speed 7 fps). The apparent streaks observed under normal flow conditions arise from the interaction between rapid RBC movement and raster scanning during image acquisition, which can elongate fluorescent signals within a single frame. As RBC velocity decreases during ischaemia and early reperfusion, individual cells become clearly resolved within the vessel lumen, eliminating this apparent streaking artefact.
