German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier has begun a three-day state visit to neighboring France. He landed at Paris' Orly airport on a damp evening and made his way to the German Embassy.

He said Europe needed to face its various security and political challenges with a mixture of the head's reason and the heart's emotion.

"Reason and heart, that's what Europe needs again today, in a time of wars and crises, in which our democracies are under attack to an extent not seen since the second World War," Steinmeier said.

He said it was a mixture of reason, or head, and heart that had made the Franco-German reconciliation after the war possible.

Steinmeier was playing on a quote from French writer Victor Hugo, who in the 19th century said that in Europe, Germany was the heart and France was the head or the reason. Steinmeier said he would leave it open to debate whether the modern-day French or Germans might agree with this particular division of anatomical labor.

What mattered, he said, was that there could be only one response to the current circumstances: "Europe must, now above all, stand up confidently and in unison for the values that made us what we are: respect for human dignity, peace, freedom, democracy."

On Thursday, Steinmeier will visit the Pantheon monument in Paris with President Emmanuel Macron, to honor the Jewish historian and resistance fighter Marc Bloch, who was murdered by Nazi Germany.

Steinmeier also intimated that a new deal on Franco-German youth cooperation might be signed amid the visit, speaking of a "promise for a new generation of German and French youth, who want to learn to understand, value and love each other."

The visit coincides with considerable  political unrest among French young people in the form of ongoing school funding protests.

Steinmeier also said prior to leaving Berlin that his state visit would be "something of a farewell tour with a truly committed European and a good friend."

Both he and French President Macron took office in 2017, and both will be leaving their posts early in 2027.

Steinmeier's post, however, is far more ceremonial and removed from daily politics than that of the French president.

Immediately following its initial parliamentary session on Tuesday, Saxony-Anhalt state legislators from the far-right AfD and far-left BSW called for the federal government in Berlin to drop sanctions on Russia over its invasion of Ukraine, as well as demanding an end to all arms deliveries and financial aid for Ukraine as Kyiv defends itself from a nearly five-year Russian invasion.

Both parties are known for their Russia "friendly" stances and their ties to the Kremlin.

They have also latched onto Moscow's claim that Berlin and other EU and NATO governments are the true "war mongers" in Ukraine, baselessly claiming that Russia is a peace-loving nation forced to defend itself against hostile neighbors.

BSW founder Sahra Wagenknecht echoed AfD co-chair Alice Weidel in labeling the initiative "a signal for peace."

"Economic sanctions against Russia must end immediately," a statement said.

Germany's federal leadership, under two chancellors, has shown steadfast — if at times cautious — support for Ukraine and demanded Russia end its killing of civilians before entering into further dialogue.

Beyond dropping sanctions, the AfD and BSW insist Germany must normalize relations with Moscow, and stop "financing the war," arguing instead that money spent helping Ukraine defend its democracy would be better spent in Germany.

State governments in Germany have no say in federal foreign and energy policy, but the parties say they launched their effort in an attempt to force change in Berlin.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Wednesday addressed the threat of political extremism to the existing social order, accusing the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) and far-left BSW parties of wanting to wipe out "the DNA of our democracy."

"Extremism," said Merz, "is forcing its way into our state legislatures with a vengeance."

Merz, speaking in Berlin, called this week’s election of an AfD politician as speaker of Saxony-Anhalt's state parliament — the first time a far-right politician has been tapped to lead a statehouse since 1945 — a "historic break."

Moreover, Merz slammed politicians — potentially from his own conservative CDU party — for casting ballots in favor of Tobias Rausch in the Saxony-Anhalt vote, saying he was "deeply concerned and somewhat appalled" when the AfD's candidate was elected to the speaker position.

Rausch received 48 votes but the AfD and BSW only have 44 between them. It is unclear from whom Rausch picked up four extra votes — he was elected in a secret ballot.

Observers assume these came from within the CDU, but Merz said "false flag" attacks also occur in politics, implying the Social Democrats, the Left party and the Greens, all of which are also represented in the state's parliament, could be attempting to weaken or sow division in the CDU.

"Anyone ‌who ​casts their vote for the AfD is ​playing the extremists' game and undermining ⁠credibility," ​said Merz. "In any ‌case, this is incompatible ​with ⁠the values of my party, ⁠the ​CDU."

Police in Oberding, Bavaria, on Wednesday warned residents in Notzinger Moos near Munich airport to remain vigilant and urged them to keep children and small animals indoors amid a spate of puma sightings.

"According to initial expert assessments," a spokesperson for the local government said, "it could be a puma."

Authorities say it is unclear where such a cat would have come from, adding that no circuses are currently operating in the area.

It is unclear what actions — if any — local police will take.

Police have reportedly received one photo of the animal, though they have not yet published it.

Authorities encouraged anyone with information on the animal, its location or origin to contact police in the city of Erding.

The Frankfurt Book Fair opened with the Czech Republic as guest of honor. The program ranges from presentations of literary works to political debates. In addition, Latin America's 50th anniversary as the first guest of honor at the fair will be commemorated on a special stage, and Chile will present its plans as the guest country for 2027.

"Our future guest country, Chile, will have a strong presence, contributing to the program with 14 authors and illustrators," said Ines Bachor, the fair's director of public relations, at a press conference. "In addition, there will be a truly broad program featuring works from a wide range of countries, from Argentina to Guatemala, including comics."

The fair will be attended by prominent literary figures, such as the French-British essayist, lawyer and novelist Philippe Sands, who will receive the German Booksellers' Peace Prize on Sunday. On Saturday, he will participate in a roundtable discussion on the possibilities of prosecuting war crimes and the destruction of ecosystems.

The organization Writers in Exile will present some of its scholarship recipients who have found refuge in Germany, such as the Chinese philosopher Zhang Xiaomao and the Afghan writer Maryam Kishawarz, who will speak about the situation of women in her country.

Belarusian Nobel Peace Prize laureate Ales Bialiatski will also take part in a debate on the repression of critical literature in his country.

Another key topic will be the impact of artificial intelligence on issues related to intellectual property and historical memory.

The "Frankfurt Calling" program of the 78th Frankfurt Book Fair will feature 120 cultural events.

On Friday and over the weekend, the fair will open its doors to the general public, with authors signing books and presenting new releases.

The question of which lawmakers besides those of the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) and populist BSW voted to elect the AfD's Tobias Rausch as parliamentary speaker in Saxony-Anhalt on Tuesday continues to fuel speculation — and outrage.

As mentioned at the start of this blog, railroad woes are one major theme in our  Germany coverage.

But here is some very positive news for once: The parliamentary transport committee has finally endorsed a plan by railway operator Deutsche Bahn (DB) to build a new line linking Hannover and Hamburg by 2050.

The project for what is one of Germany's most important transport routes is planned to cost at least €9 billion ($10 billion) and had prompted years of wrangling.

It still requires backing by the full Bundestag, or lower house of parliament, on Thursday, but that is likely to be a formality.

The new two-track line will be designed for speeds of up to 250 kilometers per hour (155 miles per hour), cutting journey times between the two cities by 18 minutes.

However, it will be some time before construction begins: The preliminary planning report that forsees submitting approval documents in 2034, with construction to start in 2036.

Renovation of the existing line between the two cities is also planned for 2029 under the agreement.

According to DB, that route is already heavily congested, carrying up to 147% of intended capacity.

The agreement was reached despite opposition from lawmakers representing constituencies affected by the new construction, including Vice Chancellor Lars Klingbeil of the Social Democrats (SPD).

US group the Backstreet Boyswill play their final of 10 concerts in Düsseldorf tonight, capping off a week of street parties and sing-alongs to celebrate the band.

"We'll take you on a trip back in time," member Howie Dorough promised.

To the rhythm of "Larger Than Life", Nick, Howie, Brian, AJ and Kevin appeared floating above the venue, crossing the Merkur Spiel-Arena on hoverboards each night.

Against the backdrop of a giant screen displaying futuristic graphics, accompanied by pyrotechnics and a laser show, the American stars have almost finished their short German residency.

The five musicians had already presented the show, Into The Millennium, at the Sphere in Las Vegas.

According to Live Nation, the event organizer, around 450,000 tickets have been sold for the performances. These 10 concerts are the only shows the band will perform in Europe on this tour.

The concert series carried the subtitle Homecoming: Live in Germany, because the band have enjoyed a loyal fan base since their early days in Germany, as the singers emphasized in Düsseldorf.

"Germany, you were our first love. You loved us before everyone else, and we loved you," Kevin Richardson said. "So many years, so many memories," Nick Carter added.

The boy band was founded in 1993 in Orlando, Florida and was part of the pop boom of the late 1990s.

The German government has said the acquisition of a majority of shares in Hamburg logistics company Zippel by China's state-owned shipping giant Cosco could pose a security threat and has blocked the sale.

The purchase of 80% of shares in Zippel by a Chinese company "would have deepened dependencies and jeopardized the resilience of Germany’s and the EU’s supply chains," an Economy Ministry spokesman said.

Speaking after a Cabinet meeting in Berlin, the spokesman said that although Germany welcomed foreign investment in general, "there may be investments that threaten the country's security."

Reports at the end of April said that the BfV domestic intelligence agency already had reservations about the takeover, which had been approved by the Cartel Office in February.

The business daily Handelsblatt reported at the end of September that the government was intending to block the sale, though this was not confirmed by Berlin at the time.

Cosco already holds a 24.99% stake in the Hamburg container terminal Tollerort.

That deal prompted a debate at the time about China's participation in critical infrastructure in Germany.

Most people in Germany consider that Chancellor Friedrich Merz is not up to the job, a new survey has shown.

In all, 67% of respondents in the survey by the Ipsos institute were either wholly or fairly in agreement with the statement "The chancellorship of Friedrich Merz has failed."

A whole 48% completely agreed on Merz's insufficiency as leader, according to the survey published in the Spiegel news magazine.

There were regional differences in assessment's of Merz's performance, with 80% of people in eastern states completely or somewhat dissatisfied compared with 64% in the west.

However, there was much less agreement on which conservative politician could replace him if he were to step down or be ousted.

The survey gave a choice between the leader of the Bavarian Christian Social Union (CSU), Markus Söder, his party colleague Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt or the premier of the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, Hendrik Wüst.

Altogether 43% of those surveyed said none of those named came into question, while some 14% responded with "Don't know."

Söder was, however, the favorite with 20% support, followed by Wüst on 16%.

The co-leader of the Social Democrats (SPD), Lars Klingbeil, says coalition partner the Christian Democrats (CDU) has questions to answer after the election of Tobias Rausch, a politician from the Alternative for Germany (AfD), as parliamentary speaker in  Saxony-Anhalt.

"The fact that the candidate of the AfD in Saxony-Anhalt, which is classified as 'confirmed right-wing extremist,' was elected as state parliament speaker with considerably more than just AfD votes raises very urgent questions," said Klingbeil, who is also deputy chancellor and finance minister.

"Electing right-wing extremists to such positions breaks with a taboo," he said.

"Not just the BSW, but also the CDU must urgently explain how this could happen."

The SPD federal parliamentary party staged a march through Berlin's Brandenburg Gate on Tuesday to protest at the election result.

The state chapter of the AfD, which won a landslide victory at September elections, is classified as "confirmed right-wing extremist" by domestic intelligence.

Rausch was elected speaker with 48-29 votes, though just 44 votes for him would have resulted if only his party (39 seats) and the populist BSW (five seats), which had pledged to back him, had voted in his favor.

The CDU parliamentary leader, Sven Schulze, had recommended that the 15 parliamentarians in his party abstain from voting, but there were only five abstentions altogether in the secret ballot. One CDU member was absent.

The other three parties represented in the legislature — the SPD, the Left Party and the Greens — had all declared their opposition to Rausch.

Schulze said he could not believe that the four extra votes for Rausch came from the ranks of the CDU, although there has been widespread speculation that this was the case.

All mainstream parties in Germany have so far officially stated that they reject any form of cooperation with the AfD in keeping with the so-called "firewall."

August Hanning, the former president of Germany's foreign intelligence service (BND), who was arrested on suspicion of espionage, is due to appear on Wednesday before an investigating judge at the Federal Court of Justice in Karlsruhe, who will decide whether he should remain in custody pending trial.

Hanning, who stepped down as head of the BND in 2005, is under investigation on suspicion of espionage-related offenses, including high treason, obtaining state secrets, attempted high treason, and acting as an agent for a foreign intelligence service.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has reaffirmed Germany's support for Israel in remarks on the anniversary of the deadly October 7, 2023 attacks led by the Palestinian militant group Hamas.

"Germany stands firmly by Israel's side. Our Israeli friends can count on our commitment to ensuring that such a crime is never repeated," Merz said.

"We stand for the security and existence of Israel — this is a lesson drawn from the Shoah [Holocaust] and part of German identity," he said.

Merz said Germany believed that the two-state solution was the best way to establish peace in the region.

He also said the German government would do all in its power to combat rising antisemitism, adding that Jews had to be able to live in Germany without fear.

Merz said his thoughts on the anniversary were with the victims and their families, survivors and former hostages.

"And we hope that the deep pain and wounds inflicted by this day — by these monstrous, barbaric acts — can heal," he said.

Several events are being held in Berlin to mark the anniversary.

The Palestinian militant group Hamas led attacks in southern Israel on October 7, 2023 in which some 1,200 people were killed and more than 250 others abducted.

The attacks triggered the ongoing war on Hamas in Gaza, which left some 74,000 Palestinians dead, most of them civilians.
