Carrot Top Breaks Silence After Suicide Attempt

Carrot Top shared his first public comments since he was hospitalized after a suicide attempt on Sept. 18: "Thank you for being there for me during this time."

Nearly a month after the comedian (real name Scott Thompson) was hospitalized in Las Vegas following a suicide attempt in Las Vegas, he spoke out for the first time, thanking fans for their support.

“I’m truly overwhelmed by the outpouring of love, support, prayers, and well wishes I’ve received over the past couple of weeks,” Carrot Top shared on Instagram Oct. 17 alongside a photo of him smiling on stage. “Every message, text, and kind thought has meant more to me than you know.”

“While I haven’t been able to respond to everyone individually, please know that I’ve felt your love and appreciate each and every one of you from the bottom of my heart,” he added. “Thank you for being there for me during this time. It truly means the world to me!”

Additionally, the 61-year-old shared that he’ll be returning to the stage on Oct. 19 to perform his nightly comedy special, which he’s hosted at the Luxor Hotel and Casino for over 20 years.

Carrot Top was admitted to the hospital on Sept. 18 after a suicide attempt.

In an update shared two days later, his rep said in a statement to TMZ that the Dennis the Menace Strikes Again star was “breathing on his own and continuing to improve, surrounded by his family and those closest to him.”

At the time, the rep also added that Carrot Top had been reading the “many messages from fans” while in the hospital, including “stories from people about how his comedy helped them through difficult periods in their own lives.”

Along with fans rallying behind him, the Luxor Hotel and Casino publicly expressed its support for the Smiley Face star.

“Scott is a beloved member of the Luxor and MGM Resorts family, and we are relieved to hear that he is recovering,” the Luxor Hotel and Casino told KLAS following Carrot Top’s hospitalization. “We are thinking of him and wish him strength as he receives the care and support he needs.”

On Sept. 26, Carrot Top was released from the hospital, his rep confirmed to NBC News.

Following his much-anticipated return to the stage on Oct. 19, the Chairman of the Board actor has almost daily shows scheduled for the rest of the year, according to the Luxor Hotel and Casino’s website.

Although Carrot Top’s comedy career has spanned nearly four decades, he’s continued to keep a rather low profile outside of his shows.

Still, he’s previously shed light on his relationship with honing his craft.

“I think you learn the craft a little bit more, you get a little more confident,” Carrot Top explained on the Whiskey Ginger podcast in 2023. “I love writing. I love trying new jokes every night. I'll try five or six things even if they eat s--t, I do them and my crew always knows—they love that part of it.”

“I hate when comics just do the same s--t forever don't try to be creative,” he added. “Eating it is part of growing as a comic.”

For a look back at Carrot Top’s comedy career, keep reading…

Carrot Top was born in Rockledge, Florida, in 1965. But before he got his iconic moniker, he was known as Scott Thomspon. As for the story behind his stage name?

“Unfortunately, I had something to do with that,” he told Allison Kugel during a 2022 appearance on her Allison Interviews web series. “It’s a blessing and a curse. Why I did it? I don’t know.  I thought the name Scott Thompson was kind of boring. Well, not kind of, it is.”

That’s why when he decided to pursue a career in comedy in the late 1980s, he wanted a name that would stick.

“Being a stage performer, I always thought I should have something fun,” he explained. “Queen Latifah was taken, and so I thought, ‘Gosh, I need something better.’ I went up to the stage one night and said, ‘Bring me up as Carrot Top.’ They said, ‘Carrot Top? Are you sure?’  I said, ‘Yes, I’m pretty sure.’ And that was it.”

Carrot Top had his big break during a 1991 appearance on Comic Strip Live followed by a performance on Star Search, a talent competition show that originally aired from 1983 to 1995 before Netflix rebooted the series in 2026.

But his 1992 appearance on Jay Leno’s The Tonight Show marked a turning point in the comedian’s career, catapulting him into mainstream stardom.

But as the performer climbed the ranks of Hollywood, he learned that with success came public scrutiny.

"If you read what people say about you, it will kill you," he told Esquire in 2015. "Don't do that. But I know some people say I'm hideous, the most disgusting, ugliest guy in the world. And then some people say, 'Oh my God, he's f--king beautiful.'”

“I've always been love or hate,” he added. “That's fine. Thank God I've had more love than hate."

While the challenges that come with fame may ebb and flow, the entertainer previously shared his determination to stay focused on his craft.

“There’s a lot of responsibility that goes in with. If you're going to be famous, it's more about the job,” he said during a November 2023 appearance on Jason Tartick’s Trading Secrets podcast. “Being famous is silly. But you don’t really find out until you probably are. And then you're like, ‘This is silly.’

It’s also important to have a sense of humor when living in the public eye. As he jokingly added, “And I’m not famous. I’m recognizable in a lineup.”

Yes, Carrot Top’s unmissable hairdo is in fact natural.

After all, he told Bill Maher during a 2023 appearance on the Club Random Podcast that his locks are “all mine.”

Though he used to rock a fire red curly afro back in the day, he now rocks a “dreaded” look, which he shared was a reflection of his mental health.

“It’s dreaded because I dread every f--king day of my life being Carrot Top. So, I dread it,” he said before adding of his words, “That’s actually funny.”

As for why he prefers for his locks to grow wild? Well, after Shania Twain asked him why he doesn’t put on a hat to cover his hair, he recalled to NPR in 2022 that “I said, No, I like my hair! I don’t wanna be stuffed into a hat.”

“I like being Carrot Top, I don’t have a problem with being Carrot Top,” he added. “I don’t mind being me.”

Though Carrot Top has had a residency at the Luxor Hotel and Casino in Las Vegas for the past 21 years, he wouldn’t consider himself one of the greats just yet.

“That means you’re old,” he told NPR in 2022, “because you can’t be an icon or a legend unless you’ve been around for a long time.”

But as he continues to climb his way there, he's dedicated to helping the young comedians coming though the door behind him.

“You just got to go do it,” he said. “Bite it, go for it and get as much stage time as you can. So you can kind of figure out what it is you are going to talk about when you get up there.”

Before Carrot Top was hospitalized in Las Vegas in September 2026, he shared insight into the future of his career, telling NPR that what would come next for him was “Probably a nap.”

But in the meantime, he shared his desire to keep performing at the Luxor.

“This is all I know,” he said. “I don’t have anything else. I’m just this. When this is it, I’ll just go sit somewhere and ponder how much fun I had.”
