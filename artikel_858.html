Interleukin-10 (IL-10) is a central anti-inflammatory cytokine that suppresses immune responses in myeloid cells but can also activate T cells, limiting its therapeutic use in chronic inflammatory diseases owing to narrow efficacy windows and dose-limiting toxicities1,2,3,4,5. Here we show that myeloid and T cells differ in their dependence on JAK-family kinase pairing for IL-10 signalling and function. We took advantage of this differential kinase dependence to engineer a tetravalent nanobody-based myeloid-selective IL-10 receptor agonist (msIL-10R agonist) that fully recapitulates IL-10’s potent suppression of monocytes, macrophages and dendritic cells while avoiding stimulation of T cells. Cryo-electron microscopy structural analysis reveals a distinct receptor cross-linking geometry of the msIL-10R agonist compared with native IL-10 family cytokines. This tetravalent architecture renders signalling strictly TYK2-dependent and highly sensitive to IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ receptor abundance, thereby conferring selective activity in myeloid cells. This msIL-10R agonist reduces inflammation in humanized mouse models of lipopolysaccharide challenge, delayed-type hypersensitivity and colitis. A humanized version exhibits IgG-like pharmacokinetics, sustained receptor occupancy and durable myeloid suppression up to 30 days without T cell activation or haematological toxicities in non-human primates. These findings establish a geometry-based strategy for achieving cell-type-selective cytokine signalling and identify the msIL-10R agonist as a therapeutic candidate for myeloid-driven inflammatory diseases, including inflammatory bowel diseases.

IL-10 is a potent anti-inflammatory cytokine that maintains immune tolerance1,6. Loss-of-function mutations in IL10 or its receptors IL10RA (encoding IL-10Rα) and IL10RB (encoding IL-10Rβ) cause early-onset inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and autoinflammatory disorders1,7. IL-10 signalling is initiated when IL-10 binds to IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ to form a tetrameric receptor complex, which is constitutively associated with Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2), respectively1,8. This binding triggers JAK1-dependent phosphorylation of IL-10Rα and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) to suppress pro-inflammatory gene expression and promote immune tolerance8,9. IL-10 primarily exerts its tolerogenic effects in myeloid cells and antigen-presenting cells, such as dendritic cells (DCs) and macrophages, by inhibiting cytokine production, antigen presentation and T cell priming5,10,11,12,13. Loss of IL10RA or STAT3 in these cells leads to colitis and systemic inflammation10,12,13.

Despite its central role in immune tolerance, the dual functionality of IL-10 severely limits its therapeutic use6,14. Whereas IL-10 potently suppresses inflammation in myeloid cells, higher concentrations activate T cells, promote IFNγ-dependent myelopoiesis and reduce platelet and erythrocyte counts2,4,5,15. Early clinical trials in Crohn’s disease and psoriasis revealed a narrow therapeutic window, with dose-limiting anaemia and thrombocytopaenia from unintended T and B cell activation5,14. Conversely, its ability to enhance cytotoxic T cell function and T helper type 1 (TH1) cell differentiation has driven the development of pegylated IL-10 as a cancer immunotherapeutic for solid tumours16. However, IL-10’s limited therapeutic index, stemming from these opposing immunosuppressive and immunostimulatory effects plus haematological toxicities, remains a major obstacle16,17,18. Previous attempts to engineer selective IL-10 variants have been constrained by short half-life, immunogenicity and incomplete separation of beneficial and harmful activities14,19,20.

Activation of JAK family kinases requires receptor-mediated dimerization that enables transphosphorylation21,22. Native IL-10 is a domain-swapped dimer19,23 that assembles a (IL-10Rα)2–(IL-10Rβ)2 signalling complex, enabling JAK1 activation through both TYK2–JAK1 and JAK1–JAK1 transphosphorylation pathways21,22,23 (Fig. 1a). To dissect these signalling routes, we used a selective allosteric JAK1 inhibitor (VVD-118313), which targets mouse C816 and selectively blocks JAK1 transphosphorylation without inhibiting kinase domains22. In bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs), JAK1–JAK1 transphosphorylation inhibition by VVD-118313 partially reduced IL-10-induced STAT3 phosphorylation but did not abrogate functional responses, consistent with compensation through TYK2-mediated transphosphorylation (Fig. 1b and Extended Data Fig. 1c–e). In TYK2-deficient BMDMs, VVD-118313 completely abrogated STAT3 phosphorylation and IL-10 function, indicating that at least one transphosphorylation route, either JAK1–JAK1 or TYK2–JAK1, must remain intact for signalling competence. As a control, the ATP-competitive JAK1 kinase inhibitor (upadacitinib) fully blocked IL-10-mediated STAT3 phosphorylation and suppression of LPS-induced TNF and IL-6 (Fig. 1b and Extended Data Fig. 1c–e).

Consistent with these findings in mouse BMDMs, pharmacological inhibition of TYK2 with zasocitinib partially reduced IL-10-driven STAT3 phosphorylation in human monocytes (Fig.1c, Extended Data Fig. 1f and Supplementary Fig. 2) yet did not impair functional suppression of lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced TNF and IL-6 in primary blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) (Fig. 1d). As a control, zasocitinib fully blocked IL-23- and IFNα-induced STAT3 phosphorylation and downstream signalling (Extended Data Fig. 2a). CRISPR-mediated TYK2 knockout in human macrophages produced similar results (Extended Data Fig. 2b,c), confirming that TYK2 is dispensable for IL-10-mediated TNF and IL-6 inhibition in macrophages24.

By contrast, both TYK2 inhibitor and CRISPR knockout diminished IL-10-induced STAT3 phosphorylation in CD8+ T cells (Fig. 1e,f and Extended Data Figs. 1g and 2d) and completely abolished augmentation of granzyme B and IFNγ production (Fig. 1g,h), indicating a strict dependence on TYK2 in T cells.

We next tested whether tuning signalling amplitude could exploit this differential kinase dependence. An engineered IL-10 monomer19 that engages a single IL-10Rα–IL-10Rβ heterodimer (Fig. 1a) restricts signalling to a single TYK2–JAK1 transphosphorylation route (Extended Data Fig. 2e–h). The monomer induced around 50% maximal STAT3 phosphorylation in blood monocytes yet retained substantial TNF suppression and partial IL-6 suppression (Fig. 1i,j). In T cells, the monomer did not induce STAT3 phosphorylation (Fig. 1i, Extended Data Fig. 1g and Supplementary Fig. 2) or increase IFNγ and granzyme B production (Fig. 1k). These data show distinct thresholds across cell types: myeloid cells tolerate attenuated input, whereas T cells require maximal signalling, enabling natural myeloid selectivity with attenuated agonists.

Collectively, these observations indicate that the natural immune cell-type-specific dependence on TYK2, combined with tuning signalling strength, can be exploited to generate selective IL-10 agonists that uncouple myeloid immunosuppression from stimulation of T cells.

Although attenuated ligands such as IL-10 monomer and affinity-tuned IL-10 muteins provide an in vitro proof of concept for myeloid selectivity20, their therapeutic potential is constrained by susceptibility to naturally occurring anti-IL-10 autoantibodies25, thermal instability, reduced potency for TNF and IL-6 suppression (Fig. 1j), and residual T and B cell activation20. To overcome these constraints and enable precise control of IL-10R signalling, we pursued a single-domain antibody (nanobody) approach. Nanobodies are well suited for receptor agonism owing to their small size (12–14 kDa), high affinity and favourable developability properties26.

Among 882 combinations tested, 182 constructs exhibited STAT3 reporter activities and 21 molecules achieved over 90% TNF and over 85% IL-6 inhibition in LPS-stimulated human PBMCs (Supplementary Fig. 3). However, most tetravalent IL-10R agonistic nanobodies recapitulated some degree of the T cell stimulatory profile of native IL-10, as evidenced by increased IFNγ and granzyme B secretion by CD8+ T cells and exemplified by a pan-IL-10R agonist (Supplementary Fig. 4), highlighting the challenge of achieving functional selectivity.

Selectivity and potency were highly clone specific. One tetravalent construct (Fig. 2c) exhibited picomolar potency together with near-complete myeloid selectivity: half-maximum inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 2.1 pM for LPS-induced TNF and 6.9 pM for IL-6 in PBMCs, with no significant induction of IFNγ or granzyme B in T cells or B cell proliferation (Fig. 2d,e and Extended Data Fig. 3a–d). We designated this molecule as the myeloid-selective IL-10R agonist (hereafter, msIL-10R agonist). Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis confirmed high-affinity binding to human IL-10Rα (KD = 2.8 nM) and IL-10Rβ (KD = 1.1 nM) with substantially higher affinity for IL-10Rβ relative to native IL-10 (Supplementary Fig. 5 and Supplementary Table 3).

The msIL-10R agonist demonstrated improved lymphocyte selectivity compared with affinity-detuned, myeloid-biased IL-10 muteins20 (Extended Data Fig. 3a–d). Whereas the IL-10 muteins 10DE and D25K retained approximately 30% maximal STAT3 phosphorylation in T cells and induced IFNγ production20, the msIL-10R agonist elicited minimal signalling (<10% Emax) in CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells (Fig. 2f, Extended Data Fig. 3a–c and Supplementary Fig. 6) and did not induce the cytotoxic effector program (Fig. 2e and Extended Data Fig. 3c). Furthermore, in contrast to IL-10 and the IL-10 muteins, the msIL-10R agonist exhibited minimal phosphorylation of STAT3 (pSTAT3) in B cells (Extended Data Fig. 3a,b) and did not promote B cell expansion or plasmablast differentiation (Extended Data Fig. 3d), demonstrating improved selectivity across lymphocyte populations.

Transcriptional profiling by RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) showed highly correlated transcriptional responses to IL-10, IL-10–Fc and the msIL-10R agonist in resting and LPS-stimulated PBMCs, including comparable suppression of inflammatory genes such as CSF3, IL6 and PTGES (Fig. 2g and Supplementary Fig. 7). By contrast, only IL-10 and IL-10–Fc induced cytotoxic T cell-associated genes (such as GZMB and BCL6) in activated T cell cultures, whereas the msIL-10R agonist induced no significantly differentially expressed genes in T cells (Fig. 2h and Supplementary Fig. 8).

Together, through empirical high-throughput screening of approximately 1,000 tetravalent nanobody assemblies, we identified an msIL-10R agonist that recapitulates IL-10’s anti-inflammatory program in myeloid cells while uncoupling its immunostimulatory effects in lymphocytes.

The msIL-10R agonist comprises tandem VHH domains fused to an Fc scaffold, with an anti-IL-10Rα VHH (clone E07) linked to an anti-IL-10Rβ VHH (clone A05) (hereafter, E07-A05) in each arm (Fig. 2c). To define its mode of receptor engagement, we determined the cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structure of IL-10Rα ECD and IL-10Rβ ECD in a complex with E07-A05 tandem VHH.

Owing to the small size of the receptor–VHH complex (around 78 kDa) for cryo-EM, we used a recently developed anti-VHH fiducial Fab (NabFab; Fab 1) to facilitate particle alignment and reconstruction27. NabFab recognizes an engineered binding epitope within VHH A05 distal to the E07-A05 linker and all CDRs and does not affect biological activities (Extended Data Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. 9). The cryo-EM map of the IL-10Rα–IL-10Rβ–E07-A05 complex was resolved to an overall resolution of 3.14 Å (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Fig. 10). The local resolution ranges from 2.8 Å at the IL-10Rβ–VHH A05 interface to about 4 Å at the IL-10Rα–VHH E07 interface, indicating conformational flexibility between the receptor-binding modules. To further define the E07 epitope, we determined a second cryo-EM structure of deglycosylated IL-10Rα ECD in a complex with E07 and an anti-VHH Fab (Fab 2) at a resolution of 2.40 Å (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Fig. 11). Integration of these datasets yielded a composite molecular model of the complete receptor-bound assembly (Fig. 3c).

Comparison with receptor complexes formed by IL-10, IL-22 and IFNλ reveals a distinct receptor organization induced by the msIL-10R agonist23,28,29 (Fig. 3c,d and Extended Data Fig. 5). VHH A05 binds to an epitope on IL-10Rβ that partially overlaps the cytokine-binding surface used by IL-10 family cytokines. However, A05 binding induces a local conformational rearrangement of the Asn138–Trp143 loop of IL-10Rβ, positioning Trp143 and Tyr140 into a cleft formed by CDR2 and CDR3 of VHH A05 (Fig. 3e). Additional contacts involving CDR2 and CDR3 generate an extensive interaction network that stabilizes receptor engagement (Fig. 3e).

By contrast, VHH E07 recognizes a large surface spanning the junction between the tandem fibronectin type III domains of IL-10Rα (Fig. 3f). This epitope is situated on the opposite side of the IL-10-binding site and is not in contact with known disease-associated IL10RA mutations linked to IBD30 (Fig. 3g). Consistent with this observation, disease-associated IL-10-binding interface mutations in IL-10Rα (R117C, R117H and Y64C) markedly impaired TNF suppression by native IL-10 and, to an even greater extent, by affinity-detuned IL-10 muteins (Fig. 3h). By contrast, the msIL-10R agonist retained full activity in cells expressing these disease-associated IL10RA variants (Fig. 3h).

To define the structural determinants for agonism, we selectively disrupted receptor engagement by introducing binding-disrupting mutations into either E07 or A05. Loss of IL-10Rα or IL-10Rβ binding, whether in cis or trans, markedly reduced STAT3 phosphorylation and impaired suppression of proinflammatory cytokines, including TNF, IL-6 and IL-1β (Extended Data Fig. 6a–c). Maximal potency and efficacy required simultaneous cross-linking of two IL-10Rα with either one or two IL-10Rβ molecules (Extended Data Fig. 6a–c), demonstrating that the tetravalent structure is essential for full agonist activity.

Importantly, agonism and myeloid selectivity were highly sensitive to molecular geometry. Altering the linker length between E07 and A05, either shortening or extending the GS linker, substantially reduced activity (Extended Data Fig. 6d). Likewise, reversing the topology of the tandem VHHs completely abolished signalling. Whereas the lead E07-A05/E07-A05 construct functioned as a potent myeloid-selective IL-10R agonist, the topologically inverted A05-E07/A05-E07 construct displayed no detectable activity in the STAT3 reporter assay (Extended Data Fig. 6e). Notably, constructs with symmetric architectures (A05-A05/E07-E07) increased pSTAT3 activity and enhanced myeloid activity, but also restored T cell activation, inducing approximately 20% of the IFNγ response elicited by native IL-10 (Extended Data Fig. 6e–g). These results indicate that signalling output and cellular selectivity are governed not simply by receptor occupancy, but by the precise topology and geometry of receptor cross-linking.

Taken together, these structural and functional studies demonstrate that the msIL-10R agonist assembles IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ into a receptor geometry distinct from that induced by native IL-10 family cytokines. This unique tetravalent assembly provides the structural foundation for the potent myeloid-selective signalling properties of the msIL-10R agonist.

We next investigated the molecular basis for myeloid selectivity of the msIL-10R agonist. In human macrophages, the msIL-10R agonist induced TYK2, JAK1 and STAT3 phosphorylation, with more sustained TYK2 phosphorylation than IL-10 at the 2 h timepoint (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Fig. 12). By contrast, in T cells, the msIL-10R agonist elicited negligible JAK1 or TYK2 phosphorylation and substantially reduced STAT3 activation, as assessed by both immunoblotting (Fig. 4b and Supplementary Fig. 13) and flow cytometry (Fig. 2g and Supplementary Fig. 8).

Notably, signalling induced by the msIL-10R agonist was strictly TYK2 dependent. Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of TYK2 completely abolished STAT3 phosphorylation and anti-inflammatory activity in macrophages (Fig. 4a,c–e). This dependency was further validated in a STAT3–luciferase reporter system, whereby msIL-10R agonist activity required the presence of TYK2 (Fig. 4f). Notably, non-myeloid-selective tetravalent IL-10R agonists, including the pan-IL-10R agonist and A05-A05/E07-E07 construct, retained partial STAT3 phosphorylation in TYK2-knockout cells (Fig. 4f), highlighting the unique TYK2 reliance by the msIL-10R agonist. Similarly, in BMDMs from human IL10RA/IL10RB-knock-in mice, TYK2 inhibition abrogated suppression of TNF and IL-6, whereas JAK1 transphosphorylation inhibitor VVD-118313 had minimal effects (Extended Data Fig. 7a–d). Together, these results indicate that the distinct receptor cross-linking geometry imposed by the msIL-10R agonist impairs productive JAK1–JAK1 transphosphorylation, rendering signalling propagation strictly dependent on TYK2 kinase activity. This mechanism provides a molecular explanation for both its reduced signalling amplitude and its myeloid selectivity.

Myeloid cells (monocytes, macrophages, DCs) express 2- to 18-fold higher surface copies of both IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ than do T, B or natural killer (NK) cells (Fig. 4g). To determine whether receptor abundance governs msIL-10R agonist selectivity, we ectopically expressed IL-10Rα and/or IL-10Rβ in activated CD8+ T cells. Increasing IL-10Rα expression to levels comparable to those observed on human monocytes (Extended Data Fig. 8a) substantially restored STAT3 phosphorylation induced by msIL-10R agonist or IL-10 monomer (Fig. 4h and Extended Data Fig. 8b–e). Co-expression of both IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ further enhanced granzyme B production by msIL-10R agonist relative to non-overexpressed control (Fig. 4h and Extended Data Fig. 8b,c). Overexpression of IL-10Rβ, TYK2 or JAK1 individually was insufficient (Extended Data Fig. 8b–i). Thus, differential IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ copy number is the critical determinant of myeloid-selective IL-10R agonism.

We reasoned that, if receptor abundance dictates selectivity, then pairing IL-10Rα with a receptor enriched on lymphocytes should redirect STAT3 agonist signalling toward T cells. IL-2Rγ (common γ chain) is expressed around tenfold higher on T and NK cells than on myeloid cells31. We therefore generated a panel of bispecific nanobodies targeting IL-10Rα and IL-2Rγ. Two lead constructs, termed T/NK IL-10R agonist, preferentially induced STAT3 and STAT1, but not STAT5, phosphorylation in CD8+ T and NK cells32, promoted granzyme B production in T cells and exhibited minimal activity in monocytes (Fig. 4i,j and Extended Data Fig. 9a–c). Similar to the msIL-10R agonist, activity was highly dependent on receptor geometry, as a symmetric tandem VHH topology completely abolished signalling despite preserving receptor-binding affinity (Extended Data Fig. 9d–f). Taken together, these data demonstrate that cell-type-specific IL-10R agonism is dictated by the relative abundance and pairing of target receptors, enabling selectivity through an engineered bispecific nanobody.

IL-10 limits tissue inflammation primarily by inhibiting cytokine production and DC maturation11,13,33. The msIL-10R agonist fully recapitulated these activities, potently blocking GM-CSF–driven monocyte into CD1a+ DCs (Fig. 5a) and suppressing zymosan-stimulated TNF and IL-23 release from DCs (Fig. 5b). In antigen-presentation assays, pretreatment of DCs with the msIL-10R agonist or IL-10–Fc strongly impaired expansion of influenza HA–tetramer+ T cells after peptide pulsing (Fig. 5c). The agonist also mirrored native IL-10 in attenuating immune-complex-triggered TNF production through FcγR signalling in macrophages (Fig. 5d). As the msIL-10R agonist lacks cross-reactivity with mouse IL-10 receptors, we evaluated its in vivo efficacy in human IL10RA/IL10RB-knock-in mice. In an LPS challenge model, msIL-10R agonist and IL-10–Fc equivalently suppressed systemic TNF, IL-6 and IL-12 production34 (Fig. 5e). In a keyhole limpet haemocyanin (KLH)/alum-induced delayed-type hypersensitivity model, the msIL-10R agonist reduced ear swelling (Fig. 5f), reduced neutrophil infiltration in draining lymph nodes (Fig. 5g) and attenuated KLH-specific IFNγ and TNF recall responses ex vivo (Fig. 5h). Similarly, in dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis (Fig. 5i), the msIL-10R agonist improved the colon weight/length ratio (Fig. 5j), attenuated systemic neutrophil expansion (Fig. 5k) and reduced tissue expression of proinflammatory genes including Tnf, Il1b and Csf3r (Fig. 5l). Collectively, these results demonstrate that the msIL-10R agonist delivers myeloid-restricted anti-inflammatory activity across multiple humanized in vivo models, faithfully recapitulating the immunoregulatory function of native IL-10.

Native IL-10 has a short serum half-life (2.7–4.5 h in humans), and long-acting variants such as PEGylated IL-10 or IL-10 Fc fusions extend exposure but drive undesirable T cell activation and haematological toxicities including anaemia/thrombocytopenia in preclinical and clinical studies2,4,6. In cynomolgus monkeys, a fully humanized version of the msIL-10R agonist exhibited dose-proportional exposure, sustained receptor occupancy on blood monocytes and a terminal half-life of approximately 8 days (Extended Data Fig. 10a,b). Ex vivo LPS challenge of whole blood from dosed animals confirmed durable suppression of TNF for more than 3 weeks after a single dose (Extended Data Fig. 10c). Importantly, whereas IL-10–Fc induced IL-18 (a reported biomarker for T cell activation and haematological toxicity in cynomolgus monkeys4,35) at 0.25 mg kg−1, and thrombocytopaenia and anaemia, the msIL-10R agonist showed no significant dose-dependent IL-18 elevation nor haematological abnormalities at doses up to 100 mg kg−1 (Extended Data Fig. 10d,e).

Thus, the msIL-10R agonist achieves prolonged myeloid-selective IL-10R agonism with an excellent safety profile, eliminating the T cell stimulation and haematotoxicity observed with IL-10–Fc.

Here we describe a fully synthetic, myeloid-selective IL-10 receptor agonist that recapitulates the potent immunosuppressive activity of IL-10 in monocytes, macrophages and DCs while uncoupling the T cell stimulatory effects that have historically limited IL-10 therapeutics.

Our studies reveal a cell-type-specific requirement for TYK2 downstream of IL-10R signalling. Whereas myeloid cells retain anti-inflammatory responses despite TYK2 deficiency and partial attenuation of STAT3 signalling, T cells exhibit a much greater dependence on TYK2 activity and maximal pathway activation. We further demonstrate that pSTAT3 signalling strength can be finely tuned through nanobody-mediated engineering of receptor geometry, thereby enabling the development of the msIL-10R agonist. The tetravalent architecture of the msIL-10R agonist imposes a distinct mode of receptor cross-linking that renders signalling strictly TYK2 dependent and highly sensitive to receptor density. Myeloid cells express substantially higher surface levels of both IL-10Rα and IL-10Rβ, enabling efficient receptor clustering and robust downstream signalling. By contrast, lower receptor abundance on T and B cells appears insufficient to achieve the avidity threshold required for productive signalling. Although receptor abundance emerged as the determinant of selectivity, additional mechanisms may contribute. T cells may possess higher signalling thresholds or enhanced negative regulatory mechanisms, including phosphatases that attenuate IL-10R signalling36, thereby further restricting responses to attenuated IL-10R agonists. Together, these findings suggest that receptor copy number and cell-intrinsic signalling thresholds cooperate to establish the natural myeloid bias observed with attenuated IL-10R signalling and provide a mechanistic framework for engineering cell-type-selective cytokine agonists37.

Previous efforts to achieve selective IL-10 signalling primarily relied on affinity tuning through partial disruption of IL-10Rβ engagement20. Although these approaches demonstrated a proof of concept for in vitro functional selectivity, residual STAT3 signalling and T cell activation were consistently observed20 (Extended Data Fig. 3). By contrast, the msIL-10R agonist achieves improved cell type selectivity between myeloid and lymphoid populations through engineered receptor geometry rather than cytokine mutagenesis, which is constrained by the native cytokine interaction interfaces. The structural basis of receptor engagement also offers advantages in disease settings in which native IL-10 signalling is compromised. A substantial fraction of patients with very-early-onset IBD have IL10RA mutations that impair ligand–receptor interactions. As the msIL-10R agonist engages epitopes spatially distinct from the native cytokine-binding interface, signalling remained preserved across multiple pathogenic IL10RA variants examined in this study (Fig. 3h). Moreover, the fully synthetic nanobody scaffold lacks sequence homology to IL-10 and would be expected to resist neutralization by endogenous anti-IL-10 autoantibodies reported in IBD25.

In summary, we identify a cell-type-specific requirement for TYK2 in IL-10R signalling that arises from differential receptor abundance and signalling thresholds across immune cell populations. By exploiting these natural differences, we engineered a precision IL-10 therapeutic that preserves full anti-inflammatory potency while substantially reducing historical on-target liabilities. The msIL-10R agonist therefore represents a differentiated therapeutic candidate for IBD and other myeloid-driven inflammatory diseases, while the underlying engineering principles provide a generalizable blueprint for cell-type-specific cytokine agonists and antagonists across immunology and oncology.
